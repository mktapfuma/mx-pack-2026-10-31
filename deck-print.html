What we found

Your website is not currently a courier website. It is a website template with a few courier words dropped in — and the template it came from belongs to a hotel and a security company.

Four things a customer feels on the other end:

  • The "Services" page sells a hotel room. It describes private rooms, a bar fridge and Netflix at a guesthouse in Vaal Dam — 804 visible characters, most of them about an accommodation booking. MEASURED, 24 October 2026.
  • The social buttons in the header go nowhere. Across 23 pages: 67 links point at "#" — 66 of them are social buttons (46 with names, 20 icons). 0 live social destinations. 0 WhatsApp links. 0 LinkedIn links. 0 email links on the site. MEASURED, 24 October 2026.
  • Your own content is missing while the template's content is live. A good, specific blog post from April 2024 exists, listed twice, with no preview image and no structure around it.
  • There is no way for a customer to check on a parcel. For a courier that is the most-requested thing a customer does. Today the answer to "where is my parcel?" is a phone call.

And the commercial cost of it, in plain terms:

  • A link you share on WhatsApp or Facebook shows as bare text — no picture, no description — in the two channels your customers actually use.
  • The site cannot answer "what does this company do?"
  • The site asks for too little on the enquiry form, so most conversations start with a phone call that re-types what the form could have asked.
  • Two names in one ecosystem — "Modern Xpress" and "Modern Express" — split the search result and the trust. The Facebook page and the website do not match by name.
  • The site carries another company's name in the footer of every page, and one live page says the website is suspended.

What we will not promise you

This list is as important as the list of features. It is what you are actually buying.

  • No chat robot on the website before there is real data behind it. A customer asking "where is my parcel" and getting a confident wrong answer is worse than no chat robot.
  • No computer-sent reply to a customer without a person reading it. In logistics, a wrong committed date is a commercial breach.
  • No model writes your prices, your transit times or your coverage. Those are commercial and legally binding statements, and they stay human-owned.
  • No invented reviews, testimonials or performance figures. Fake customer evidence is fraud.
  • No arrival-date prediction early. An invented estimate destroys the trust the rest of the work builds. It is a later phase, gated on measured accuracy against your own transit times.
  • No legal or compliance conclusion. What the Zambian Data Protection Act requires of your business is your lawyer's determination, and we will build the controls to point at the sections so their review has something specific to review.
  • No change of scope by conversation. A change is priced in a new written document and agreed before it is built.

The AI part, in plain terms

AI is not a chat robot, and it is not the thing that fixes the website. It is what makes the operation cheaper to run after the site, the channels and the tracking exist.

What it does, in order of what it is worth to you:

  • Enquiry triage — sorts every enquiry into "rate", "where is my parcel", "complaint", "collection", "partner enquiry", pulls out the origin, destination, size and date, routes it to the right queue, starts the clock, and drafts a first reply for a person to read. Starts as soon as the enquiry form is live.
  • Quote assist — turns "3 boxes of clothes, Johannesburg to Lusaka, before the 20th" into a structured request with an indicative rate band from your own rate card, with the assumptions written out, for a person to confirm. Starts when you give us your rate card and service list. It never sends and never commits a price.
  • Document reading — reads a proof of delivery, a waybill or an invoice and pulls the fields out, checks them against the consignment record, and flags anything that does not match. Starts once your document flow exists. The minutes it saves are the first-pass reading minutes, not the whole task — a person still checks and files. That review time is a real running cost and we name it in the price rather than pretend it away.
  • Exception alerts, arrival estimates and a customer self-service assistant — these all need the tracking system first. They are later phases, and we will not sell them to you as if they were available now.

The accuracy test is agreed before we run it. For document reading: a labelled sample of 100 real documents, a floor agreed in advance, and a test that fails the release if accuracy falls below it. A threshold set after seeing the result is not a threshold.

Your data is not used to train or fine-tune anything. Retrieval-based grounding only, and we propose that as a written term, not a preference.

What it could be worth, in your numbers

We are not going to tell you what this saves. We do not know your numbers, and you do.

So the pack gives you a worksheet you fill in — six figures you already have — and it tells you how many consignments a month have to move through the new enquiry route for the fee to pay for itself. The number you get is one you can defend to your own boss, because you produced it.

Six inputs: enquiries per month · the share of enquiries lost or delayed because the answer was not instant or complete · revenue per won consignment · the share of consignments lost to a competitor who answered faster · the minutes it takes to give a customer a rate · the cost of answering one enquiry by hand.

And the sentence that matters, said out loud:

> "If the number you get is larger than your monthly volume, this is not worth doing yet — and I would rather tell you that now than take your money."

No AI savings are folded into that number. The AI programme is a separate sequence with its own price. Mixing the two is the first thing a procurement team catches, and it is how a good proposal loses trust.
