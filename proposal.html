1 · The short version

Your website is currently a template with courier words dropped in. The first piece of work makes the site say what the business does, makes the enquiry route work, and gives every shared link a preview. After that, the channels and the tracking become possible — but only in that order, and only with your inputs.

  • Phase 1 (2–4 weeks) — the site: template residue out, your business written on every page, the enquiry route rebuilt, link previews, accessibility, and a written acceptance record for each of it.
  • Phase 2 (6–8 weeks) — the channels: the website and the Facebook page as one system, one brand, one enquiry route.
  • Phase 3 (discovery 1–2 weeks, then 8–12 weeks) — tracking: a customer-facing consignment lookup, and a manager's view of the vehicles.
  • The AI programme — separate, later, and priced separately. It makes the operation cheaper to run after the site, the channels and the tracking exist.

4 · The AI programme, in plain terms

AI is not a chat robot, and it is not the thing that fixes the website. It is what makes the operation cheaper to run once the site, the channels and the tracking exist.

What it does, in order of what it is worth to you

  • Enquiry triage. Sorts each enquiry into "rate", "where is my parcel", "complaint", "collection" or "partner enquiry"; pulls out the origin, destination, size band, service and date; routes it to the right queue; starts the clock; drafts a first reply for a person to read. Starts as soon as the enquiry form is live and the basic setup is done.
  • Quote assist. Turns "3 boxes of clothes, Johannesburg to Lusaka, before the 20th" into a structured request with an indicative rate band from your own rate card, assumptions written out, for a person to confirm. Starts when we hold your rate card, service list, lane list and size bands. It never sends and never commits a price.
  • Document reading. Reads a proof of delivery, a waybill or an invoice, pulls the fields out, checks them against the consignment record, and flags anything that does not match. Starts once your document flow exists — the first-pass reading only; a person still checks and files. The minutes this saves are the first-pass reading minutes, not the whole task, and that review time is a real running cost we name in the price.
  • Exception alerts, arrival estimates, self-service lookup. All three need the tracking system first. Later phases, and we will not sell them to you as if they were available now.

The things we will not do

  1. No chat robot on the site before there is real data behind it. A confident wrong answer is worse than no answer.
  2. No computer-sent reply to a customer without a person reading it. In logistics, a wrong committed date is a commercial breach.
  3. No model writes your rate card, prices, transit times or coverage claims.
  4. No invented reviews, testimonials or performance figures.
  5. No personal data of a driver or recipient fed into a model because it was convenient.
  6. No special personal information, and no biometric or emotion inference on drivers.
  7. No training or fine-tuning on your data. Retrieval-based grounding only, proposed as a written term, not a preference.

How accuracy is claimed

The threshold is agreed before the result is seen: a labelled sample of 100 real documents, an accuracy floor agreed in advance, and a test that fails the release if accuracy falls below it. The floor applies to the lower bound of the confidence interval, not to a round number we happen to hit — on a hundred documents a 95% floor is only claimable if every one of the hundred is right. The sample is held back and never used to tune the prompts. Accuracy is reported per field and per document class, because one pooled number hides a class that fails.

5 · How we prove it

  • A 12-point acceptance baseline for the site, measured against your own pages, plus gates for content and for channel resolution.
  • Each gate is re-measured against a named dated record with a checksum — not by moving the ruler during the check.
  • A channel baseline is pinned before the first change. The figure that settles acceptance is the count of links that moved against that baseline.
  • We show you the records. Every number in this pack is re-runnable by you or by anyone else with the same tool.
  • No invoice before acceptance. The evidence accompanying the invoice is the evidence the gate rests on.

7 · What we will not promise you

  1. No chat robot before there is real data behind it.
  2. No computer-sent customer reply without a person reading it.
  3. No model writes your prices, transit times or coverage.
  4. No invented customer evidence, ever.
  5. No arrival-date prediction early — an invented estimate destroys the trust everything else builds. It is gated on measured accuracy against your own transit times.
  6. No legal or compliance conclusion. What the Zambian Data Protection Act requires of your business is your lawyer's determination; we build the controls to point at the sections so their review has something specific to review.
  7. No scope change by conversation. A change is priced in a new written document, agreed before it is built.
  8. No prepayment buys future work. A deposit, if one is agreed, buys no exclusivity and no promise of a later phase.

8 · How the value works

We do not know your enquiry volume, your margin or your desk cost — you do. So we have built a worksheet you fill in: six figures you already have, in your currency, producing the number of consignments a month that have to move through the new enquiry route for the fee to pay for itself.

"If the number you get is larger than your monthly volume, this is not worth doing yet — and I would rather tell you that now than take your money."

No AI saving is folded into that number. The AI programme is a separate sequence with its own price, and mixing the two is the first thing a procurement team catches.
